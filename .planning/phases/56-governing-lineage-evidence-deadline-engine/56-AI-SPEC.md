## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Structured Data Extraction followed by deterministic local lineage adjudication and civil-date/deadline evaluation

**Description:**
Phase 56 adds one bounded model-assisted stage that examines one freshly authorized source at a time and proposes closed candidates for execution/effect evidence, explicit lineage language and scope, typed contract facts, and data-only deadline rules. The model receives only engine-issued handles and locator-preserving excerpts. It cannot compare the corpus, rank documents, calculate a date, select a governing term, issue an alert-eligibility decision, create durable identities or URLs, or execute tools or expressions.

The trusted background validates each response as hostile data, maps every handle and evidence range back to the current Phase 55 generation, and either accepts the whole candidate batch or abstains. A separate deterministic JavaScript pipeline then consumes one authority-certified complete graph set, derives all durable IDs locally, preserves conflicts, adjudicates governing lineage, evaluates allowlisted civil-date rules, and emits a cited proof or closed blocker codes. Only those deterministic, input-version-bound results may become an active truth snapshot.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Model output or source prompt injection establishes a governing/eligible boolean, precedence rank, computed deadline, executable operator, tool call, URL, or durable identity.
2. An uncited, clipped, stale, cross-source, cross-partition, unknown-key, or non-plain candidate reaches deterministic adjudication or durable state.
3. A partial or unavailable source set is treated as complete, or filename, recency, similarity, folder/list order, confidence, or majority vote resolves lineage or a fact conflict.
4. Deadline arithmetic uses model output, `Date.parse`, browser locale, implicit UTC, an unstated boundary, or a business-day calendar that was not explicitly supplied and version-bound.
5. A cancelled, timed-out, provider-binding-changed, or authority-stale response stages data; raw prompts, excerpts, rejected output, or repair payloads survive the no-storage lifetime.
6. An unresolved lineage/fact conflict is silently collapsed and allows a downstream deadline to become alert-eligible.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Commercial contracting and legal operations, specifically post-signature vendor-agreement administration and contract decision support

**User Population:** In-house commercial counsel, legal-operations and contract-management practitioners, procurement/vendor managers, and accountable business owners who need to know which instrument and clause currently control and which notice action may be due

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** The durable result can influence which contract language an operator treats as governing, which lifecycle and notice facts they rely on, and whether a later phase may schedule a deadline alert. A false clearance can cause reliance on an unsigned or superseded instrument, an ineffective or misdirected notice, loss of a renewal/termination right, avoidable renewal, dispute, or commercial loss. This remains evidence-backed decision support: it neither gives legal advice nor makes an autonomous legal judgment or sends a notice.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Execution and effectiveness**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** The result distinguishes signature/execution evidence from effectiveness and from present temporal status. `Executed` is supported by the exact current evidence a reviewer would inspect; missing evidence remains unknown or review-required, and a newer unsigned draft never displaces an executed instrument merely because of its date or location.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** “Latest,” “final,” a filename, a signature-looking label, or the absence of a signature candidate is treated as proof that a document is executed, effective, unsigned, or governing.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Repository requirements TRUTH-02 through TRUTH-04 and the locked Phase 56 governing-lineage decisions; ACC's contract-management maturity model identifies incomplete execution, weak signature controls, and multiple versions as material early-stage failures.

**Dimension: Governing instrument and clause-level amendment chain**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** A governing conclusion reproduces the explicit cited base, amendment, replacement, and supersession path over the complete current authorized family. A partial amendment changes only its exact target clauses, preserves untouched base clauses as inherited, and abstains when scope, target, ordering, or execution/effectiveness is unclear.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Recency, filename, folder order, similarity, or model confidence selects a winner; an `amends` mention is treated as a whole-agreement replacement; or a partial amendment silently rewrites unrelated clauses.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Repository requirements TRUTH-02 through TRUTH-04 and Phase 56 context/research; WorldCC describes trusted contract data as requiring hierarchy, version control, governance, currentness, and enough applicability context to support decisions.

**Dimension: Exact lifecycle and notice facts with source proof**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Signed, effective, expiration, termination, renewal, notice-window, notice-deadline, delivery-method, and written-address facts remain separate typed assertions. Each material value opens to the exact accessible revision, record/locator, and byte range that supports it, and source access/currentness is reported separately from whether the claim was extracted, inferred, ambiguous, unreadable, or review-required.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A lifecycle summary collapses unlike dates; a notice address or method is paraphrased beyond the clause; a citation points only to a filename/document rather than the operative location; or a stale/inaccessible revision continues to support a claim.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Repository requirements TRUTH-06, TRUTH-07, and TRUTH-09; ACC practitioner guidance emphasizes precise answers to who/what/where/when/how and a detailed schedule of contract deadlines.

**Dimension: Conflict handling and professional abstention**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Every incompatible applicable assertion remains visible in a cited conflict set. The system issues a closed review-required/ineligible outcome whenever the evidence set is incomplete, inaccessible, unreadable, conflicting, unsupported, or not current, and the result explains exactly what must be resolved.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Majority vote, confidence, source count, recency, or “most plausible” wording hides a conflict; the system fills a missing fact; or provisional synthetic labels are presented as expert-approved truth.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Repository requirements TRUTH-04 and TRUTH-11, Phase 55's unresolved expert UAT, NIST AI 600-1's treatment of confabulation/information integrity, and ABA Formal Opinion 512's requirement for task-appropriate independent verification when lawyers use GAI.

**Dimension: Notice and deadline-rule fidelity**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** The derivation names the exact governing anchor fact, notice-window rule, day unit, inclusivity/exclusivity, timezone, any immutable business-day calendar, consequence of inaction, and resulting civil date. A second practitioner can reproduce it without browser locale or unstated assumptions, and eligibility remains blocked when any input or semantic is unresolved.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Renewal, expiration, termination, and notice deadline are conflated; the model supplies arithmetic; calendar and business days are interchanged; or month-end, holiday, timezone, and boundary behavior is silently assumed.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Repository requirements TRUTH-07, TRUTH-08, and TRUTH-11 plus the locked Phase 56 deadline decisions; ACC guidance notes that missed written-notice deadlines can forfeit contractual options and recommends maintaining detailed deadline schedules.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **The latest-looking copy wins.** A renamed draft, redline, or recent upload is mistaken for the executed/effective agreement, displacing the actual governing paper.
2. **Amendment overreach.** A clause-scoped change is applied to the whole agreement, an amendment target is guessed, or multiple amendments are ordered without explicit admissible lineage.
3. **Citation and authority drift.** A correct-looking fact remains attached to a replaced, revoked, inaccessible, cross-account, or wrong-corpus source, or navigation reaches the document but not the evidence that supports the claim.
4. **Deadline compression.** The operator sees an expiration or renewal date as though it were the notice deadline, while the notice method, destination, day type, boundary, timezone, or consequence is omitted or approximated.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

This engine is not a jurisdiction-neutral substitute for contract interpretation. Execution, effectiveness, amendment scope, notice sufficiency, and enforceability can depend on the complete agreement and applicable law. Phase 56 therefore encodes only the approved closed evidence and date-rule subset, labels unsupported meaning review-required, and leaves legal conclusions and action to qualified reviewers. No jurisdiction-specific legal rule is inferred from the external sources below.

Where lawyers use the system in legal practice, [ABA Formal Opinion 512](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf) is relevant professional guidance—not a universal software regulation. Deployment counsel must determine which professional rules, client instructions, consent duties, privilege controls, privacy laws, records policies, and contractual restrictions actually apply.

The contract corpus may contain confidential, privileged, personal, security-sensitive, or commercially sensitive information. Evaluation must preserve the repository's current-authority, account/corpus isolation, least-excerpt, configured-provider, no-wholesale-upload, no-raw-output-storage, and source-revocation boundaries. [NIST AI 600-1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) is voluntary risk-management guidance; its emphasis on confabulation, content provenance, domain-expert validation, adversarial testing, and real-world evaluation supports these controls but does not confer legal compliance.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Existing direct `UniversalProvider` with a dedicated closed-schema `skopeo-truth-extractor`; no new orchestration or RAG framework

**Version:** FSB v0.9.90 provider contract and the repository-pinned JavaScript runtime

**Rationale:**
Phase 56 has one narrow, source-local model call whose output remains candidate-only. The repository already owns configured provider routing, cancellation, bounded requests, raw-response lifetime, no-storage acknowledgement, and exact provider-binding checks. Governing lineage, conflicts, civil-date arithmetic, and deadline eligibility are deterministic local code and do not benefit from an agent, workflow graph, or RAG abstraction.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangChain.js 1.x structured output | Adds MV3 bundle and abstraction overhead while duplicating provider routing, retries, cancellation, validation, and lifecycle controls already present; reconsider only if provider-parity evaluations prove a concrete gap. |
| LlamaIndex | Retrieval/RAG is outside this phase; Phase 55 already provides the authorized graph input and search cannot prove exact-set completeness. |
| LangGraph or another agent framework | The flow is linear and the authoritative steps must remain deterministic; model orchestration would enlarge the trust boundary without adding required capability. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — preserve the configured model-agnostic provider path (xAI, OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, OpenRouter, custom OpenAI-compatible endpoints, or LM Studio).

### Entry Point Pattern

```javascript
// Planned internal extractor pattern. This function runs inside one fresh
// Phase 54 ingestion operation and has no durable write capability.
async function extractTruthCandidateBatch({
  settings,
  operationSignal,
  promptVersion,
  batchNonce,
  excerpts,
  engineHandles,
  validationContext
}) {
  if (operationSignal.aborted) throw new Error('SKOPEO_TRUTH_CANCELLED');

  const provider = new UniversalProvider(settings);
  const prompt = {
    systemPrompt: STATIC_TRUTH_SYSTEM_PROMPT,
    userPrompt: JSON.stringify({
      schemaVersion: 1,
      promptVersion,
      batchNonce,
      excerpts,
      engineHandles
    })
  };

  const body = await provider.buildRequest(prompt, {});
  if (operationSignal.aborted) throw new Error('SKOPEO_TRUTH_CANCELLED');
  if (body.generationConfig) {
    body.generationConfig.temperature = 0.1;
    body.generationConfig.maxOutputTokens = 2048;
  } else {
    body.temperature = 0.1;
    body.max_tokens = 2048;
  }

  let wireResponse = null;
  let rawResponse = null;
  try {
    wireResponse = await provider.sendRequest(body, {
      attempt: 0,
      timeout: 20000,
      signal: operationSignal
    });
    if (operationSignal.aborted) throw new Error('SKOPEO_TRUTH_CANCELLED');

    const parsed = provider.parseResponse(wireResponse);
    rawResponse = parsed && parsed.content;
    if (parsed && typeof parsed.model === 'string' &&
        parsed.model !== settings.modelName) {
      throw new Error('SKOPEO_TRUTH_PROVIDER_BINDING_CHANGED');
    }
    if (typeof rawResponse !== 'string' || rawResponse.length === 0 ||
        rawResponse.length > 131072) {
      throw new Error('SKOPEO_TRUTH_MODEL_RESPONSE_BOUNDS');
    }

    // No fence stripping, JSON repair, confidence threshold, or fallback.
    const decoded = JSON.parse(rawResponse);
    const candidate = await FsbSkopeoTruthSchema.parseCandidateEnvelope(
      decoded,
      validationContext
    );
    if (!candidate) throw new Error('SKOPEO_TRUTH_MODEL_CANDIDATE_INVALID');
    if (operationSignal.aborted) throw new Error('SKOPEO_TRUTH_CANCELLED');
    return candidate;
  } finally {
    // The enclosing engine additionally requires the Phase 55
    // provider-no-storage acknowledgement before returning from the operation.
    rawResponse = null;
    wireResponse = null;
  }
}
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|------------|-----------------|
| `UniversalProvider` | Existing configured-provider formatter, fetch/cancellation/retry boundary, and raw response normalizer | Build and send each bounded source-local candidate request using exactly the current `modelProvider` and `modelName`. |
| `skopeo-truth-extractor` | Dedicated stateless candidate stage with static prompt, finite budgets, provider-binding checks, and no-storage acknowledgement | Propose typed evidence from one authorized source; never perform cross-document resolution. |
| `skopeo-truth-schema` | Closed Draft 2020-12 shapes plus descriptor-safe semantic parsing, evidence resolution, canonicalization, and locally derived IDs | Reject hostile/unknown data and normalize only candidates whose issued handles, locators, types, and versions exactly match current input. |
| Exact-set graph snapshot | New private graph query/engine operation over the entire already-authorized source set | Supply a complete, sorted, capped, current input to deterministic adjudication; lexical search is never a substitute. |
| Lineage adjudicator and deadline engine | Pure, storage-free, model-free functions over closed data | Resolve four lineage axes, overlays, applicability/conflicts, civil dates, proof objects, and blocker codes reproducibly. |
| Truth store/facade | Pointer-last immutable snapshots, reverse dependencies, purge ownership, and minimized fresh-authority projections | Persist accepted deterministic proof and expose only bounded lineage/fact/conflict/citation/deadline views to later phases. |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Parsing Phase 55 labels as typed truth.** `record.label` is bounded model text, not a signed date, amendment scope, address, or deadline rule. Add a dedicated closed Phase 56 candidate contract.
2. **Treating JSON Schema success as semantic authority.** Draft validation does not prove descriptor safety, `start < end`, issued-handle ownership, byte-range resolution, current generations, endpoint compatibility, or exact-set completeness. Run the trusted semantic parser after schema validation.
3. **Asking the model to compare documents or calculate dates.** The model is source-local and candidate-only. Governing lineage, conflicts, applicability, civil-date arithmetic, and eligibility belong only to deterministic code.
4. **Assuming one request shape across providers.** Gemini uses `generationConfig.maxOutputTokens`; current OpenAI-compatible and Anthropic shapes expose `max_tokens`. Apply caps after `buildRequest()` and retain provider-parity fixtures.
5. **Leaking rejected output through repair or diagnostics.** A repair receives only bounded category and JSON-pointer paths, never the rejected response. Logs retain no prompts, excerpt text, fact values, citations, filenames, source IDs, addresses, or raw provider errors.
6. **Racing instead of cancelling.** `Promise.race` alone leaves network/backoff work alive. Pass the Phase 54 signal into `sendRequest`, recheck after every `await`, and make delayed results incapable of staging.
7. **Relying on service-worker memory.** Chrome may terminate an MV3 worker. Persist only validated immutable pages/journals, withdraw active pointers first, and make recovery reconstruct currentness from durable bindings.
8. **Using semantic cache hits as evidence.** Reuse is permitted only for the exact partition/source/content-generation/schema/prompt/provider/model tuple under fresh authority.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**

- Use exactly the current `config.getAll()` values for `modelProvider` and `modelName`; never select a vendor/model, create a key, fall back, or route a subtask elsewhere.
- After `UniversalProvider.buildRequest()`, set temperature to `0.1` and the provider-specific output cap to 2,048 tokens. Sampling is not an authority control; strict validation remains mandatory if an endpoint removes an unsupported optional parameter.
- Keep the Phase 55 initial envelope: at most 8 excerpts and 24,000 source characters per normal call, at most 8 normal calls/192,000 source characters per source generation, at most 1 repair call, a 20-second provider timeout, and at most 131,072 raw response characters. Tighten only through versioned evaluation evidence; never silently raise a cap.
- Require one bare JSON object. Do not request provider-native tool calls, function calls, code execution, URLs, arbitrary expressions, confidence, `governing`, `eligible`, a computed date, or provider-generated durable IDs.
- Bind the generation to truth schema/prompt version and the exact configured provider/model. A response naming a different model, a settings change between steps, or an absent provider configuration invalidates the generation.

**Core Pattern:**

1. Obtain the complete current source selection from the background-owned visible corpus manifest and enter a fresh exact-set Phase 54 operation. If any source is unavailable, stale, or over cap, emit a closed blocker; never model or adjudicate a prefix.
2. Consume the private Phase 55 exact-set snapshot. The graph layer must reparse and sort every current source record, local relation, and endpoint-current candidate overlay and return a complete-input digest with no raw text, filename, URL, lexical score, cache, certificate, or storage handle.
3. For each source needing Phase 56 candidates, read its certified content through the existing transport, normalize and segment locally, and create only engine-issued document/clause/evidence handles. The model call remains source-local even though the later adjudicator receives an exact set.
4. Build a static, versioned system prompt and a `JSON.stringify` user-data envelope. Perform one configured-provider call inside one fresh ingestion operation; pass its signal through fetch and backoff.
5. Bound the raw string, parse JSON exactly once, validate a closed Draft 2020-12 envelope, then run descriptor-safe semantic parsing. Resolve every response-local handle/locator against the issued registry and exact graph/source generation. Reject the whole batch on any unknown key, collision, stale endpoint, invalid range, unsupported value/rule, or ownership mismatch.
6. Drop the raw response before leaving the provider-no-storage operation. A single new-authority repair is allowed only for JSON/schema shape failure and receives only bounded error codes/paths. Semantic, evidence, provider-binding, cancellation, and authority failures are not repairable.
7. Feed only normalized frozen candidates plus the certified exact-set snapshot and explicit as-of/timezone/calendar inputs into pure adjudication. The adjudicator forms families only from explicit current relations/evidence, preserves conflicts, and either returns a fully cited four-axis proof or abstains.
8. Evaluate only closed deadline operators over validated civil dates. The result separates anchors, window start, deadline, boundary, timezone, consequence, and eligibility blockers.
9. Persist immutable normalized candidates/proof pages bound to every input generation and rule version, validate reverse dependencies, then publish one active family pointer last. Recheck exact authority and graph/truth bindings before publication and on every facade read.

**Tool Use:**

None. Do not give the model browser actions, MCP tools, storage, graph/query capabilities, fetch/URL access, callbacks, `eval`, `Function`, SQL, JMESPath, arbitrary rule operators, or source navigation. The only external effect is the already-configured provider request. The only accepted output is inert closed data; source navigation later takes a locally derived citation ID and performs a fresh background `citation-open` operation.

**State Management:**

- Model calls are stateless. Do not use automation conversation history, response cache, compaction, a second model pass, or an agent session.
- Keep the source/excerpt registry and raw response in a nonserializable in-memory extraction session only for the active certified step. Explicitly clear raw response references after parsing; persist no prompt, full source text, rejected output, or provider error body.
- Durable state consists only of validated, canonical, locally identified candidate/assertion/citation/conflict/lineage/deadline pages; versioned journals; source-to-family reverse dependencies; and validated active controls in `chrome.storage.local`.
- Withdraw affected family pointers synchronously before source purge, graph replacement, or candidate-overlay replacement. Stage new immutable pages invisibly, validate hashes/dependencies/input versions, and publish pointer-last.
- On MV3 recovery, validate controls/pages/journals in bounded sorted passes and leave uncertain data invisible. The first authorized read compares the complete persisted input binding with a fresh exact-set snapshot and withdraws on mismatch.

**Context Window Strategy:**

This is segmented extraction, not RAG. Send only one source's current ordered locator-preserving excerpt batch, the closed vocabulary, and the minimum engine-issued handles needed to refer to records/clauses already validated for that source. Do not send another document, corpus summaries, graph search hits, filenames, folder order, prior raw responses, or a retrieved few-shot corpus.

Across batches, expose only a capped handle/kind projection; never replay full earlier text or model output. Maintain a local coverage ledger bound to the certified fingerprint. If the source cannot be covered inside the fixed call/character budget, return pending/review-required and publish no partial candidate generation. Cross-document comparison happens only after extraction, in deterministic code over the complete exact-set snapshot.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

Pydantic is illustrative only. Phase 56 runs in a packaged Chrome MV3 JavaScript service worker and must not add Python, Pydantic, an `instructor` bridge, a server, or a provider-specific `response_format` dependency. The following model shows the intended closedness for one candidate subtype; it is design documentation, not production code. Pydantic defaults to ignoring unknown fields, so the illustration explicitly uses `extra='forbid'` and strict validation.

```python
from typing import Annotated, Literal

from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field, StringConstraints

ClosedText = Annotated[
    str,
    StringConstraints(min_length=1, max_length=96, pattern=r"^[A-Za-z0-9._:-]+$"),
]
CivilDate = Annotated[
    str,
    StringConstraints(pattern=r"^[0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}$"),
]


class EvidenceRef(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid", frozen=True, strict=True)

    excerpt_id: Annotated[str, StringConstraints(min_length=1, max_length=64)]
    start: Annotated[int, Field(ge=0, le=24_000)]
    end: Annotated[int, Field(ge=1, le=24_000)]


class CivilDateCandidate(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid", frozen=True, strict=True)

    candidate_ref: ClosedText
    fact_type: Literal[
        "signed-date",
        "effective-date",
        "expiration-date",
        "termination-date",
    ]
    value: CivilDate
    evidence: Annotated[list[EvidenceRef], Field(min_length=1, max_length=4)]


class TruthCandidateEnvelope(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid", frozen=True, strict=True)

    schema_version: Literal[1]
    batch_id: ClosedText
    civil_dates: Annotated[list[CivilDateCandidate], Field(max_length=128)]
```

```javascript
const CIVIL_DATE_CANDIDATE_SCHEMA = {
  type: 'object',
  additionalProperties: false,
  required: ['schemaVersion', 'batchId', 'civilDates'],
  properties: {
    schemaVersion: { const: 1 },
    batchId: { type: 'string', pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9._:-]{1,96}$' },
    civilDates: {
      type: 'array',
      maxItems: 128,
      items: {
        type: 'object',
        additionalProperties: false,
        required: ['candidateRef', 'factType', 'value', 'evidence'],
        properties: {
          candidateRef: {
            type: 'string',
            pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9._:-]{1,96}$'
          },
          factType: {
            enum: [
              'signed-date',
              'effective-date',
              'expiration-date',
              'termination-date'
            ]
          },
          value: {
            type: 'string',
            pattern: '^[0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}$'
          },
          evidence: {
            type: 'array',
            minItems: 1,
            maxItems: 4,
            items: { '$ref': '#/$defs/evidence' }
          }
        }
      }
    }
  },
  '$defs': {
    evidence: {
      type: 'object',
      additionalProperties: false,
      required: ['excerptId', 'start', 'end'],
      properties: {
        excerptId: { type: 'string', pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64}$' },
        start: { type: 'integer', minimum: 0, maximum: 24000 },
        end: { type: 'integer', minimum: 1, maximum: 24000 }
      }
    }
  }
};

const candidateValidator = new CfworkerJsonSchema.Validator(
  CIVIL_DATE_CANDIDATE_SCHEMA,
  '2020-12',
  false
);

async function parseCivilDateCandidateEnvelope(value, issuedContext) {
  // These private helpers follow skopeo-graph-schema.js: no getters,
  // symbols, prototypes, sparse arrays, cycles, or unknown own keys.
  if (!descriptorSafeTree(value)) return null;
  const validation = candidateValidator.validate(value);
  if (!validation || validation.valid !== true) return null;

  const envelope = dataValues(value, ['schemaVersion', 'batchId', 'civilDates']);
  const inputs = envelope &&
    dataArrayValues(envelope.civilDates, 128, 0);
  if (!envelope || envelope.schemaVersion !== 1 || !inputs) return null;

  const parsed = [];
  const seen = new Set();
  for (const input of inputs) {
    const fields = dataValues(
      input,
      ['candidateRef', 'factType', 'value', 'evidence']
    );
    if (!fields || seen.has(fields.candidateRef)) return null;

    // This checks real Gregorian validity (not regex alone), resolves every
    // range against an engine-issued excerpt, and binds current source/graph
    // versions. It returns null on any stale, clipped, or foreign locator.
    const civilDate = parseCivilDate(fields.value);
    const evidence = await parseIssuedEvidenceList(
      fields.evidence,
      issuedContext
    );
    if (!civilDate || !evidence) return null;

    const candidateId = await deriveTruthCandidateId({
      issuedContext,
      factType: fields.factType,
      civilDate,
      evidence
    });
    if (!candidateId) return null;
    seen.add(fields.candidateRef);
    parsed.push(deepFreeze({
      candidateId,
      factType: fields.factType,
      typedValue: { kind: 'civil-date', value: civilDate },
      evidence
    }));
  }
  return deepFreeze({ schemaVersion: 1, candidates: parsed });
}
```

Validation is fail closed. Allow at most one repair for bare-JSON or closed-schema shape errors, under a new certified operation. Log only a fixed category and at most 16 bounded JSON-pointer paths. Never echo the rejected output. Descriptor/evidence/semantic/currentness/provider-binding/cancellation failures are nonrepairable and leave the whole candidate generation unpublished.

Pydantic references for the illustration: [models and `model_validate`](https://docs.pydantic.dev/latest/concepts/models/) and [strict mode](https://docs.pydantic.dev/latest/concepts/strict_mode/).

### Async-First Design

Use browser promises and `await`; Python event-loop guidance does not apply. Every content read, provider call, rate-limit wait, parse boundary, snapshot read, staged mutation, and final publication receives or is fenced by the Phase 54 operation signal. Recheck cancellation and the exact live tuple immediately after every `await`.

One common mistake is racing an uncancellable provider promise. The existing `UniversalProvider.sendRequest(..., { signal })` composes caller cancellation into fetch, response consumption, backoff, and retry; preserve that path. A late completion must have no mutation capability and must fail the provider-no-storage/currentness handshake.

Await the complete response. Streaming is inappropriate: a token prefix or partial JSON cannot be schema-checked, evidence-resolved, provider-binding-checked, or atomically accepted. Parallel source calls are allowed only if each has its own fresh certified operation and bounded budget; deterministic adjudication starts after the complete exact set and all accepted source generations are fixed.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- Keep immutable extraction policy, closed vocabulary, forbidden decisions, and output contract in a versioned system message. Put all source-derived bytes and engine-issued handles in one `JSON.stringify` user-data envelope.
- State that contract text, comments, filenames, quoted instructions, and prompt-like content are inert data and cannot change policy, call tools, request URLs, add fields, rank documents, calculate dates, or declare governance/eligibility.
- Request one bare object and enumerate every candidate kind, fact type, lineage effect/scope, date-rule operator, boundary, unit, and trust-adjacent field. Still parse the response as hostile input.
- Use no dynamic few-shot retrieval. If evaluation justifies examples, add at most a small static synthetic set to the trusted system prompt and bump the prompt version; never use customer/source text or another corpus.
- Set source-character/excerpt/call limits and provider-specific output tokens explicitly. Never leave production output unbounded, and never ask the model to fill missing evidence from general knowledge.

### Context Window Management

Do not add RAG, embeddings, reranking, or corpus summarization. Phase 55 already supplies exact identity/provenance, and relevance search cannot prove completeness. Candidate extraction processes stable, ordered, locator-preserving batches from one certified source; cross-document lineage is resolved later by deterministic code over the exact-set snapshot.

Carry only a bounded projection of prior engine-issued handles between batches. Do not resend prior raw responses, conversation history, other sources, graph labels for similarity, or provider memory. Track coverage locally against the exact content fingerprint. If a document exceeds the fixed per-source budget, abstain with a closed blocker and publish nothing rather than truncate or summarize away a potentially operative clause.

### Cost and Latency Budget

Per source generation, the hard ceiling is 8 normal calls plus 1 schema-repair call. Each call includes at most 24,000 source characters and requests at most 2,048 output tokens, so source payload is capped at 192,000 characters and requested output at 18,432 tokens, plus fixed prompt/handle overhead. Each provider call uses a 20-second timeout and its own fresh authority operation. Deterministic graph snapshotting, parsing, hashing, lineage, conflict resolution, and civil-date arithmetic consume no model tokens.

A fixed dollar estimate would be misleading because the user controls the provider/model and may use a custom or local endpoint. Record only the existing safe provider/model identifiers, supplied token usage, duration, attempt/repair counts, and fixed outcome categories; compute currency only where the repository already has a current price entry.

Reuse only a fully validated candidate generation under the exact partition/source/content fingerprint/fragment generation/schema/prompt/provider/model tuple and fresh authority. Semantic caching is forbidden. Do not route classification, repair, summarization, or any other subtask to a cheaper or fallback model; perform all non-extraction work deterministically in local JavaScript.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

Phase 56 uses product-level extraction and deterministic-decision evaluations, not general model benchmarks. The release gate executes synthetic or irreversibly redacted cases against production schema, extractor, adjudicator, deadline, store, and runtime modules. It reports three independent statuses:

1. `deterministic_structural_security: pass|fail` — closed-schema admission, evidence/currentness, deterministic rules, authority, persistence, and privacy.
2. `provisional_regression: pass|fail (not gold)` — exact comparison with pending synthetic/redacted expected outcomes.
3. `domain_fidelity: human_needed|approved` — representative governing-lineage, clause-scope, fact, notice, and deadline labels approved by real counsel and legal-operations reviewers with versioned evidence.

The first two statuses can pass while the third remains `human_needed`. No aggregate, confidence score, optional provider run, or automated judge may convert pending expert evidence into domain approval. Every Critical structural/security fixture is a hard gate; averages cannot compensate for one false governing conclusion, one false eligible deadline, one stale citation, or one privacy escape.

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** No external tracing or telemetry. Use only the extension's existing bounded background diagnostic mechanism with fixed, content-free enum counters and metadata-only ring entries inside the local extension boundary. Arize Phoenix/OpenTelemetry and hosted or self-hosted trace services are deliberately prohibited: prompts, responses, contract facts, addresses, citations, and source identifiers cannot leave the extension, and Phase 56 adds no service or monitoring infrastructure.

**Key Metrics to Track:**

The exact-key diagnostic shape is limited to: diagnostic schema version; operation class; fixed outcome/blocker code; truth schema, prompt, adjudicator, and deadline-rule versions; attempted/accepted/published/withdrawn counts; duration bucket; retry/repair count; recovery-state code; and a coarse timestamp. **Alert Thresholds:**

These thresholds create only a local diagnostic/release-blocking state; they do **not** create Chrome alarms, notifications, recipients, or Phase 59 alert records.

| Threshold | Required response |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Any lifetime value above zero for unsafe clearance, eligibility with blockers, conflict collapse, partial/stale publication, late write, absence-proof failure, unsupported operator execution, nondeterminism, privacy/raw-lifetime/boundary escape, or scheduling effects | Critical: withdraw/keep affected truth invisible, stop clearance, add a synthetic regression, and block release until the focused and full gates pass. |
