## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Extraction

**Description:**
Phase 55 adds bounded, model-assisted extraction of graph records from one freshly certified source at a time. The feature runs only in the trusted MV3 background, sends only permission-scoped locator-preserving excerpts through the user's already configured FSB provider, validates closed-schema output against the certified source, and publishes only a complete provenance-bound replacement.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Source or page content changes instructions, triggers tools, follows URLs, or otherwise escapes the inert-data boundary.
2. A graph record is published without a valid source locator, current authority, closed-vocabulary type/relation, or verifiable provenance.
3. A cancelled, malformed, quota-failed, or partially extracted replacement becomes visible and leaves stale facts, relations, index entries, or consequences behind.
4. More source text than the bounded, permission-scoped excerpt set is sent to the configured provider.
5. Provider configuration is bypassed or a new external runtime, framework, credential, service, or corpus-wide upload is introduced.

## 1b. Domain Context

**Industry Vertical:** Commercial contract intelligence for in-house legal operations, procurement, and vendor-management review

**User Population:** Contract managers, legal-operations and procurement reviewers, vendor owners, and supervising commercial counsel who must verify the source before relying on extracted intelligence

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** Phase 55 publishes evidence-bound graph candidates and search/traversal indexes that later phases may use to analyze agreement lineage, surface exact terms, and support deadlines or alerts. It does not provide legal advice, decide which document governs, or take legal action. A false, cross-matter, or stale candidate can nevertheless misdirect a reviewer or contaminate later analysis, while an over-broad provider request can expose confidential contract information.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Operative-clause evidence fidelity**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Every record and relation resolves to an exact span in the one supplied source, and the span includes the language needed to understand the proposition, including any nearby negation, exception, qualifier, or incorporated reference. If the supplied excerpts do not contain sufficient operative language, no candidate is published.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A label is supported only by a heading, recital, truncated sentence, unmatched offset, or text that omits a material carve-out; a plausible contract term is supplied from model knowledge instead of the source.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** The expert-annotated CUAD contract-review task treats the output as salient contract text for human review, not an uncited legal conclusion; NIST identifies confident false content and invented supporting citations as consequential confabulation risks.

**Dimension: Amendment identity without a precedence claim**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** An amendment remains a distinct source-owned record and links to an agreement or clause only when cited language identifies the target. The relationship means only “amends-candidate”; it does not assert effectiveness, supersession, partial displacement, or present governance.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** The engine fuses an amendment into a base agreement, links documents because their titles or parties look similar, or treats date, filename, folder order, or recency as proof of governing precedence.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** Commercial-contract review practice requires legal judgment to resolve document effect; ABA Formal Opinion 512 says generative tools cannot replace the professional judgment required for legal work.

**Dimension: Matter and source isolation**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Extraction receives only bounded excerpts from the freshly authorized source in the active account/corpus partition; every output reference resolves within that supplied excerpt set, and caches, logs, and queries cannot reveal another source or partition.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A candidate contains a party, clause, or relationship learned from another contract, a revoked source, provider memory, or an unauthorized partition, or diagnostics retain contract text beyond the authorized operation.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** ABA Formal Opinion 512 requires lawyers to assess disclosure and unauthorized-access risk before sending client information to a generative tool; NIST AI 600-1 identifies leakage and unauthorized use or disclosure of sensitive data as a generative-AI privacy risk.

**Dimension: Revision-safe withdrawal**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** When a source changes, is deleted, or loses authorization, its prior fragment, postings, edges, cache influence, and downstream consequences are absent before any replacement becomes visible; the replacement is either complete for the new fingerprint or withheld.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Old and new generations coexist, search or traversal still returns a deleted clause, or a failed replacement leaves the earlier version looking current.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** FSB requirements `CORPUS-05` and `TRUTH-05`; where GDPR applies, Article 5's accuracy, storage-limitation, and integrity/confidentiality principles reinforce controlled correction and removal.

**Dimension: Independent expert reviewability**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** A reviewer can inspect the exact source identity and fingerprint, open each locator, distinguish record and relation types, see that the generation is current and complete, and reach an independent conclusion without relying on model confidence.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** The engine presents a polished summary, confidence score, or “cleared” state without inspectable evidence, hides missing coverage, or makes the reviewer infer which source version produced the result.  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** ABA Formal Opinion 512 calls for task-appropriate independent verification of generative-AI output and retains human responsibility; CUAD defines contract extraction as highlighting evidence important for a human to review.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Qualification clipping:** extraction captures a renewal, assignment, notice, or termination phrase but drops a nearby exception, defined term, cross-reference, or negation, changing the commercial meaning while preserving fluent wording.
2. **Document-family conflation:** similarly named agreements, amendments, vendors, or repeated clause headings are merged or cross-linked, creating a plausible but unsupported document lineage.
3. **Stale dual truth:** a changed, deleted, or access-revoked source continues contributing facts or relationships after a replacement attempt, so reviewers see historical language as current.
4. **Confidentiality boundary failure:** contract text, party identity, or derived intelligence crosses account/corpus boundaries, is sent beyond the minimum authorized excerpt set, or survives in logs/provider payload reuse after the operation.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

**Direct engineering constraints adopted for this product:** Treat agreement content and derived records as confidential, need-to-know data; reauthorize at ingestion and use; isolate account/corpus/source state; minimize provider excerpts; retain no raw prompt or rejected output; preserve exact provenance and review state; and withdraw inaccessible or superseded source generations. These are product safeguards even when no particular statute applies. NIST AI 600-1 is voluntary risk-management guidance, not a legal classification, but supports testing for confabulation, privacy leakage, information integrity, provenance, and human over-reliance.

**Deployment- and jurisdiction-dependent obligations:** If a lawyer uses the system in a representation, the controlling jurisdiction's professional-conduct rules, client instructions, and engagement terms determine duties concerning competence, confidentiality, communication, supervision, and review; ABA Formal Opinion 512 is authoritative professional guidance but not a universal statute. If excerpts contain personal data and the processing falls within GDPR scope, Articles 5 and 32 may add purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality, and security obligations. Privilege, work-product protection, records schedules, sector rules, provider terms, lawful basis, consent, data-processing agreements, and deletion duties depend on the organization, data, provider, and jurisdiction and require counsel/privacy review.

This extraction foundation is not classified here as a legal-advice system or an autonomous decision-maker: it does not determine governing precedence, clear a decision, draft or send a notice, or act on a contract. Any regulatory classification for a later deployment must be assessed against that deployment's actual use and consequences.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Direct existing FSB `UniversalProvider` / `AIIntegration` adapter; no external AI framework

**Version:** Repository-pinned Phase 55 implementation on the existing bundled provider contract

**Rationale:**
This is a bounded, single-source structured-extraction pipeline rather than an agentic or RAG system. Direct use of FSB's existing provider adapter preserves MV3 browser compatibility, provider choice, cancellation, redaction, and permission boundaries with the smallest trusted runtime surface and no new infrastructure.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangChain.js structured-output components | Adds bundle and browser-compatibility risk for a closed-schema operation already supported by the project adapter; reconsider only if the direct validation contract proves insufficient. |
| LlamaIndex | Optimized for retrieval/RAG, while Phase 55 requires deterministic bounded extraction and local graph publication without a new runtime. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — Phase 55 is provider-agnostic at the FSB adapter boundary and uses only whichever provider the user has already configured.

## 3. Framework Quick Reference

### Installation and trusted load order

No AI-framework package is added. Phase 55 reuses the repository-pinned browser implementation:

- `config.getAll()` supplies the user's existing `modelProvider`, `modelName`, endpoint, and credential settings in the background.
- `ai/ai-integration.js` already loads `ai/ai-providers.js`, which loads `ai/universal-provider.js` and exposes `UniversalProvider`/`createAIProvider` as classic-script globals.
- `lib/cfworker-json-schema.min.js` is the existing IIFE bundle of `@cfworker/json-schema@4.1.1`. It must load before any graph schema/extractor module.
- New graph modules remain background-only classic IIFEs with CommonJS test exports. Load schema, store, extractor, then query/facade after the Phase 54 corpus chain; do not add them to any content-script dependency closure.

For a clean checkout, `npm ci` installs the already-declared validator dependency. `npm run build:cfworker` is only needed when intentionally rebuilding the checked-in validator bundle; Phase 55 should not change or replace that dependency.

### Current adapter call shape

`UniversalProvider` is the correct low-level boundary for extraction. `AIIntegration.getAutomationActions()` and its queue/CLI parsing path are not: they add automation conversation history, response caching, CLI repair, and fallback behavior that is inappropriate for a stateless closed-JSON extraction.

```js
async function requestClosedJson(settings, prompt, extractionSchema) {
  const MAX_RESPONSE_CHARS = 128 * 1024;
  const provider = new UniversalProvider(settings);
  const requestBody = await provider.buildRequest({
    systemPrompt: prompt.systemPrompt,
    userPrompt: prompt.userPrompt
  }, {});

  // buildRequest formats provider-specific bodies before returning.
  if (requestBody.generationConfig) {
    requestBody.generationConfig.temperature = 0.1;
    requestBody.generationConfig.maxOutputTokens = 2048;
  } else {
    requestBody.temperature = 0.1;
    requestBody.max_tokens = 2048;
  }

  const response = await provider.sendRequest(requestBody, {
    attempt: 0,
    timeout: 20000
  });
  const parsed = provider.parseResponse(response);
  if (typeof parsed.content !== 'string' ||
      parsed.content.length === 0 ||
      parsed.content.length > MAX_RESPONSE_CHARS) {
    throw new Error('SKOPEO_GRAPH_MODEL_RESPONSE_BOUNDS');
  }

  // No markdown stripping, JSON repair, or permissive fallback.
  const candidate = JSON.parse(parsed.content);
  const validator = new CfworkerJsonSchema.Validator(
    extractionSchema,
    '2020-12',
    false
  );
  const validation = validator.validate(candidate);
  if (!validation || validation.valid !== true) {
    throw new Error('SKOPEO_GRAPH_MODEL_SCHEMA_INVALID');
  }
  return { candidate, usage: parsed.usage, model: parsed.model };
}
```

This is the existing provider signature. Before production extraction uses it, Phase 55 must add caller-signal composition described in Section 4; the current `sendRequest` path only owns its timeout controller.

### Specific pitfalls

1. **Do not route extraction through the automation parser.** `AIIntegration.callAPI()` returns raw text for downstream CLI interpretation, and `getAutomationActions()` can cache, retry, and carry conversation state. Phase 55 needs a fresh stateless `UniversalProvider` call and its own strict JSON gate.
2. **Generation fields differ after provider formatting.** Gemini uses `generationConfig.maxOutputTokens`; the other current request shapes use `max_tokens`. Set the cap after `buildRequest()` and test every configured provider shape.
3. **`parseResponse()` intentionally does not repair JSON.** Do not revive deleted JSON cleaners or accept fenced/prose output. Bound the raw string, call `JSON.parse` once, and reject on any parse error.
4. **The current provider timeout is not an operation cancellation signal.** `fetchWithTimeout()` creates its own `AbortController`, while 429/503 backoff can retry internally. Phase 55 must compose the authority `operationSignal` into fetch and backoff checks before using this path.
5. **Schema success is not publication authority.** JSON Schema cannot prove locator text, source fingerprint, relation endpoint compatibility, candidate-reference uniqueness, or current Drive access. Run descriptor-safe semantic validation and a final authority check before staging/publishing.
6. **The bundled validator annotates its schema.** `CfworkerJsonSchema.Validator` adds internal absolute-reference metadata during construction. Do not freeze the schema object first; construct the private validator at module initialization, then expose only a closed validation function or immutable API.
7. **MV3 memory is ephemeral.** Never rely on a provider promise, in-memory batch array, or cache as the recovery record. Persist only bounded validated staging state and keep active pointers unchanged until the complete source replacement is proven.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

### Provider and generation contract

- **Provider/model:** use exactly `settings.modelProvider` and `settings.modelName` returned by `config.getAll()`. Do not select a “recommended” model, switch to a cheaper model, create credentials, or fall back to the legacy xAI path.
- **Sampling:** set temperature to `0.1` after provider formatting. If a configured endpoint rejects that parameter and `UniversalProvider` removes it through its existing unsupported-parameter retry, validation still remains mandatory; determinism must never depend on sampling alone.
- **Output:** cap each response at 2,048 output tokens and 128 KiB of raw response text. Require one bare JSON object with no prose, markdown fence, tool call, URL request, or executable field.
- **Timeout/cancellation:** keep a single provider fetch below the existing 30-second corpus authority deadline (20 seconds is the initial call timeout). Extend the existing `sendRequest(requestBody, options = {})` implementation with an optional `options.signal`, compose it with the internal timeout controller, check it before/after rate-limit sleep and before retry, and preserve all existing callers. This is a Phase 55 compatibility extension, not an API that exists today.
- **Tools:** none. Do not pass tool definitions, callbacks, browser functions, MCP handles, storage capabilities, or a URL-fetch surface.

### End-to-end extraction and publication

1. **Withdraw first.** Under the Phase 54 ingestion/mutation guard, withdraw the changed source's active graph pointer, purge `fragments`, `indexes`, `relationships`, and `result-cache` influence, and prove absence. Create an invisible source-generation staging manifest keyed by partition, source ID, content fingerprint, schema version, prompt version, configured provider, and configured model.
2. **Read and segment locally.** Use the already-certified `FsbSkopeoDriveCorpusTransport.readContent(input, operationSink, signal)` path. Deterministically normalize line endings and segment into locator-preserving excerpts. Source text, filename, and comments stay data; none can modify the system prompt.
3. **Enforce hard budgets.** Initial limits: at most 8 excerpts and 24,000 excerpt characters per provider call, 8 normal extraction calls and 192,000 excerpt characters per source generation, one additional repair call for the source, 128 candidate records, and 256 candidate relations per response. A source that cannot reach deterministic coverage inside those caps remains withheld/pending; never publish a “best effort” subset.
4. **Certify every call.** One authority callback contains at most one provider call. Each batch, including a repair attempt, starts a fresh `beginOperation('ingestion', context)` / `runWithCertifiedSource(...)` cycle so Phase 54 proves authority immediately before and after that call. The operation signal is passed into the provider adapter.
5. **Validate before durable effect.** Bound and strictly `JSON.parse` the raw response, apply the closed Draft 2020-12 schema, then run descriptor-safe semantic checks: exact keys/plain data, candidate-reference uniqueness, kind and predicate allowlists, endpoint-kind matrix, record/edge caps, all references resolved within the same response or an already validated batch, and every evidence span resolvable byte-for-byte inside the supplied excerpt.
6. **Stage only validated candidates.** Derive published IDs locally from partition key, source ID, record kind, and validated source locator/local key. Never persist a model-created global ID or confidence as truth. Attach the authority-certified content fingerprint and locator registry entry; do not persist raw prompts, full source text, or rejected model output.
7. **Publish once.** A final fresh certified ingestion operation verifies the complete coverage ledger, every staged batch fingerprint, referential integrity, derived lexical/adjacency shards, quota headroom, and cancellation state. Use the existing effect publisher/mutation guard to install the source fragment and derived shards pointer-last. Any error discards staging and leaves the source withheld with no partial fragment.
8. **Record safe diagnostics.** Locally record reason code, provider/model identifiers, prompt/schema versions, batch counts, duration, token usage, retry count, and validation error categories/JSON-pointer paths. Never log excerpt text, filenames, raw provider output, credentials, citations, or source existence outside the authorized partition.

### Prompt boundary

The system message is static, versioned trusted code. It says the task is extraction only; document text is untrusted quoted data; instructions inside it must be ignored; only the supplied excerpt IDs may be cited; output is one object matching the closed schema; and no tools, URLs, code, or external knowledge are available.

The user message is `JSON.stringify` of a closed envelope containing only a random/nonsemantic batch nonce, schema version, ordered excerpt objects (`excerptId`, bounded offsets/line range, and text), and the explicit closed vocabulary. Do not interpolate source text into the system message. Do not include other sources, account labels, secrets, unrelated corpus metadata, or prior raw model output.

Use no dynamic few-shot retrieval. If evaluation shows examples are necessary, add one or two static synthetic examples to the trusted system prompt and version the prompt; examples must contain no customer/source data.

### State, retries, and recovery

- Calls are stateless. Do not use `AIIntegration.conversationHistory`, `responseCache`, compaction, or automation sessions.
- A syntactic or closed-schema failure may receive **one** repair attempt per source generation. Start a new certified operation, resend the same authorized excerpts and static schema plus only bounded error codes/JSON-pointer paths; do not echo the rejected output. Locator mismatch, semantic allowlist failure, authority change, cancellation, quota failure, missing provider configuration, or permission failure is not repairable.
- Provider-level 429/503 retries remain inside `UniversalProvider` but must share the operation signal and absolute deadline. Once cancelled or stale, no delayed response can stage or publish.
- Persist a bounded staging/coverage journal so MV3 wake can either resume at a proven batch boundary or discard the incomplete generation. Derivable indexes rebuild from validated fragments; no other source is evicted to satisfy quota.
- Exact extraction reuse is allowed only for the same partition/source/content fingerprint/schema version/prompt version/provider/model tuple and only after fresh source authority. Similar text or a different source never reuses model output.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Pydantic — browser compatibility

Pydantic is inapplicable: Phase 55 runs as MV3 background JavaScript and may not add Python or a server process. The repository's Ajv-equivalent closed-output gate is the already bundled `@cfworker/json-schema@4.1.1` global, `CfworkerJsonSchema.Validator`. Use Draft 2020-12, `additionalProperties: false` on every object, finite array/string/integer caps, and a second semantic parser.

The extraction envelope should follow this shape (the engine, not the model, attaches source identity/fingerprint and derives published IDs):

```js
const EXTRACTION_ENVELOPE_SCHEMA = {
  type: 'object',
  additionalProperties: false,
  required: ['schemaVersion', 'batchId', 'records', 'relations'],
  properties: {
    schemaVersion: { const: 1 },
    batchId: { type: 'string', pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{16,64}$' },
    records: {
      type: 'array',
      maxItems: 128,
      items: {
        type: 'object',
        additionalProperties: false,
        required: ['candidateRef', 'kind', 'label', 'evidence'],
        properties: {
          candidateRef: {
            type: 'string',
            pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9._:-]{1,96}$'
          },
          kind: {
            enum: [
              'agreement', 'amendment', 'clause', 'fact',
              'event', 'owner', 'policy-document', 'memo'
            ]
          },
          label: { type: 'string', minLength: 1, maxLength: 1024 },
          evidence: { '$ref': '#/$defs/evidenceList' }
        }
      }
    },
    relations: {
      type: 'array',
      maxItems: 256,
      items: {
        type: 'object',
        additionalProperties: false,
        required: [
          'fromCandidateRef', 'predicate', 'toCandidateRef', 'evidence'
        ],
        properties: {
          fromCandidateRef: {
            type: 'string',
            pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9._:-]{1,96}$'
          },
          predicate: {
            enum: [
              'contains', 'amends-candidate', 'states-fact',
              'records-event', 'assigned-owner',
              'references-policy', 'references-memo'
            ]
          },
          toCandidateRef: {
            type: 'string',
            pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9._:-]{1,96}$'
          },
          evidence: { '$ref': '#/$defs/evidenceList' }
        }
      }
    }
  },
  '$defs': {
    locator: {
      type: 'object',
      additionalProperties: false,
      required: ['excerptId', 'start', 'end'],
      properties: {
        excerptId: {
          type: 'string',
          pattern: '^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64}$'
        },
        start: { type: 'integer', minimum: 0, maximum: 24000 },
        end: { type: 'integer', minimum: 1, maximum: 24000 }
      }
    },
    evidenceList: {
      type: 'array',
      minItems: 1,
      maxItems: 4,
      items: { '$ref': '#/$defs/locator' }
    }
  }
};

const validator = new CfworkerJsonSchema.Validator(
  EXTRACTION_ENVELOPE_SCHEMA,
  '2020-12',
  false
);
const result = validator.validate(candidate);
if (!result || result.valid !== true) {
  // Retain only bounded keyword/instance paths for local diagnostics.
  throw new Error('SKOPEO_GRAPH_MODEL_SCHEMA_INVALID');
}
```

After schema validation, enforce the typed endpoint matrix: `contains` is document-to-clause; `amends-candidate` is amendment-to-agreement/clause and never means governing; `states-fact` targets fact; `records-event` targets event; `assigned-owner` targets owner; and policy/memo references target their named kinds. Also require `start < end` and exact source-substring resolution, reject duplicate/dangling candidate references, and assign final deterministic IDs locally. Model confidence is neither requested nor accepted.

Validation is fail closed. One fresh-authority repair attempt is allowed only for parse/schema failures. Log error category and bounded validator paths, not values. A second failure or any semantic/locator/authority failure leaves the whole source generation unpublished.

### 4b.2 Async-first design

Use browser promises and `await` throughout; Python event-loop guidance does not apply. Pass the authority `operationSignal` through content reads, provider fetch, rate-limit waits, staging, and publication. Recheck it immediately after every awaited boundary. The common failure to avoid is merely racing an uncancellable provider promise: that stops waiting but still spends tokens and can produce a delayed result. Compose the signal into `UniversalProvider.fetchWithTimeout` so abort terminates the fetch and prevents retries.

Await the complete response. Streaming is inappropriate because no token or partial JSON is trusted before strict parse, schema validation, locator verification, and atomic source publication.

### 4b.3 Prompt engineering discipline

- Keep immutable extraction policy/schema instructions in the system message and all source-derived bytes in a delimited/JSON-encoded user-data envelope.
- State explicitly that filenames, comments, contract clauses, and quoted text cannot issue instructions, select tools, request URLs, change the schema, or authorize graph effects.
- Require one bare JSON object and enumerate record kinds/predicates in the prompt; still treat the response as hostile.
- Use only static synthetic few-shot examples if evaluation proves they help. Never dynamically retrieve examples from another source or account.
- Set both input character/excerpt caps and provider-specific output-token caps. Character/byte caps are authoritative because provider tokenizers vary.

### 4b.4 Context-window management

This is deterministic segmented extraction, not RAG. Build stable locator-preserving excerpts locally, order them by source position, and include only the current batch. Maintain a local coverage ledger keyed to the certified content fingerprint. Do not send prior batches, other sources, corpus summaries, conversation history, or graph search results back to the model.

When the source exceeds the per-call window, process sequential bounded batches into invisible staging. When it exceeds the per-source call/character budget, mark the source pending/too-large and publish nothing. Do not summarize or truncate silently, because either can erase a clause while still producing plausible graph records.

### 4b.5 Cost and latency budget

Initial hard ceiling per source generation is 8 normal calls plus at most 1 repair call. Each call carries at most 24,000 excerpt characters (approximately 6,000 tokens, tokenizer-dependent) and requests at most 2,048 output tokens. Therefore the maximum modeled budget is approximately 54,000 input tokens and 18,432 output tokens per source generation; the typical four-batch source budget is approximately 24,000 input and 8,192 output tokens.

A fixed dollar estimate would be false because the user controls provider/model and custom/local endpoints may have different pricing. Record the actual `parsed.usage`, model, provider, duration, and retry count in existing local analytics/diagnostics; calculate currency only where FSB already has a current price entry.

Cache only a fully validated exact-fingerprint result under the complete partition/source/fingerprint/schema/prompt/provider/model key and reauthorize before reuse. Semantic caching is forbidden: similar text cannot establish source identity or provenance. Do not route subtasks to a cheaper model or automatic fallback; perform segmentation, deduplication, hashing, enum checks, and routing deterministically in local JavaScript.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

Phase 55 uses product-level extraction evals, not general model benchmarks. The mandatory gate is deterministic: synthetic contract excerpts, recorded hostile/valid provider responses, scripted authority changes, and an in-memory `chrome.storage.local` harness exercise the production modules. An optional provider-quality run may send only the same synthetic fixtures through the user's already configured FSB provider; it is never allowed to require a new provider or credential.

### Evaluation tooling and release commands

The repository has an npm-driven Node assertion harness (`assert`, `vm`, production CommonJS exports, fake Chrome storage, and injected await failures) and no Jest package. Phase 55 must extend that existing infrastructure rather than add an evaluation framework or runtime dependency:

- Commit immutable JSON/JavaScript fixtures under `tests/fixtures/skopeo-graph-evals/`, with a fixture manifest that gives every case a stable ID and gold-label version.
- Add focused production-module harnesses for extraction/schema behavior, adversarial boundaries, replacement/recovery, query/provenance, and diagnostic privacy. Reuse the seeded failure-order, fake-storage, worker-recreation, and cancellation patterns in the Phase 54 corpus tests.
- Add an npm script named `test:skopeo-graph-evals` that runs those focused Node harnesses, and include it in the normal `npm test` chain. The focused implementation-time command is `npm run test:skopeo-graph-evals`; the CI/release gate is `npm run test:skopeo-graph-evals && npm test`.
- Do not add Arize Phoenix, OpenTelemetry, Promptfoo, RAGAS, LangSmith, Langfuse, Braintrust, Jest, Python, or a hosted trace/eval service. In particular, this phase explicitly overrides the evaluation workflow's Phoenix default: Phoenix requires a tracing service/Python-style runtime and conflicts with the accepted browser-local, no-new-service product boundary. No install command is required beyond the repository's existing `npm ci`.

Optional live model qualification runs inside the extension against synthetic fixtures only and uses the current `config.getAll()` provider/model path. Absence of provider configuration produces the expected pending/withheld outcome and never prompts for setup. CI remains deterministic and network-free.

### Measurable rubrics

| Dimension | Priority | PASS | FAIL | Measurement and threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operative-clause evidence fidelity / field accuracy | Critical | Every accepted record and relation matches the expert-labeled kind, predicate, endpoints, and exact supplied evidence span, including nearby qualifier, exception, negation, and cross-reference text; insufficient evidence abstains. | A missing material qualifier, unsupported field/relation, heading-only support, invented fact, or accepted locator mismatch. | Code compares published records to gold spans and exact expected sets; domain experts own labels. Deterministic gate: **100% exact evidence precision, 100% expected-set match, and 0 unsupported publications** across all positive/qualification fixtures. Optional configured-provider run: **100% evidence precision, >=90% gold-record recall, >=95% kind/predicate accuracy, and 100% abstention on insufficient-evidence cases**. |
| Closed-schema and bare-format validity | Critical | One bounded bare JSON object passes Draft 2020-12 closed schemas and exact-key/plain-data parsing; all arrays, strings, integers, references, and endpoint pairs stay within finite caps. | Prose/fences, malformed JSON, unknown keys, accessors/prototypes, oversized values, duplicate/dangling references, or an invalid endpoint reaches staging. | Code only. **100% of valid boundary fixtures accepted; 100% of invalid and max-plus-one fixtures rejected before durable effect; 0 partial records staged or published.** |
| Amendment identity and semantic restraint | Critical | An amendment remains source-owned and emits only evidence-backed `amends-candidate`; ambiguous or title-only cases abstain and never claim governing effect. | Identity fusion, filename/title/date similarity linking, or any effectiveness, supersession, displacement, or governing-precedence claim. | Code plus counsel-labeled gold relations. **100% exact match on amendment fixtures and 0 forbidden precedence predicates/fields anywhere in schema, prompt, persisted state, or query output.** |
| Matter/source isolation and safety | Critical | Each call contains only the authorized source's bounded excerpt envelope; every output reference resolves in that envelope and all storage/query paths stay in the certified partition. | Cross-source/cross-partition data, corpus-wide text, source instructions, tools/URLs/code, fallback provider, or sensitive payload data influences or survives the operation. | Code, hostile marker fixtures, and static trusted-boundary scans. **0 cross-source or cross-partition acceptances, 0 model-selected tool/network effects, 0 forbidden marker bytes in storage/log snapshots, and 100% rejection of injection/fallback fixtures.** |
| Revision-safe withdrawal and atomic task completion | Critical | A valid generation publishes exactly one complete fragment pointer-last; cancellation, authority loss, quota failure, crash, corruption, or stale generation leaves no partial/new visibility, and changed/deleted/revoked truth is absent before replacement. | Old and new truth coexist, a failed generation looks current, a delayed response mutates state, or any old posting/edge/cache result survives its required purge. | Code with an assertion at every awaited storage/publication boundary and fresh-worker replay. **100% convergence across all seeded before/after failure points, 0 late writes after cancellation, 0 stale query hits, and exactly one active generation only after complete success.** |
| Provenance reviewability and bounded query correctness | High | Exact lookup, lexical search, bounded traversal, and provenance inspection return deterministic, partition-scoped projections whose records resolve to the current source fingerprint and locator. | A hit lacks current provenance, traversal exceeds its cap, results differ after cache rebuild/restart, or raw shards/records cross the facade. | Code snapshot tests. **100% expected ordered results before/after MV3 recreation, 100% current locator resolution, 0 over-cap results, and byte-identical deterministic IDs for the same source generation.** |
| Provider, budget, and minimization adherence | Critical | Only the configured `UniversalProvider` path is called, at most once per freshly certified batch, with <=8 excerpts and <=24,000 excerpt characters; a generation uses <=8 normal calls, <=192,000 excerpt characters, and <=1 repair call. Responses stay <=2,048 requested output tokens and <=128 KiB raw text. | Automatic model/provider fallback, extra repair/retry after cancellation, corpus-wide upload, or any cap overrun. | Code spies at the provider boundary and boundary-value fixtures. **All exact-max cases follow the documented outcome, all max-plus-one cases make 0 provider/publication effects, and every attempted call is accounted for once.** |
| Independent reviewability / user-facing safety | High | The engine exposes evidence and an honest published, pending, withheld, or failed state without model confidence or legal-clearance language; a reviewer can independently inspect provenance. | A polished confidence score, hidden coverage gap, legal conclusion, or “cleared/governing” status substitutes for evidence. | Code scans of closed schemas/facade projections plus expert review of the fixture contract. **0 confidence/clearance/governing fields or claims; 100% negative cases return the gold fail-quiet state and reason category.** |

All Critical deterministic thresholds are release blockers; averages cannot compensate for one unsafe fixture. A valid but empty/abstaining result counts as task completion only when the gold fixture also requires abstention. A model response that fails a guard may be safely withheld, but it still counts as a model-quality failure in an optional configured-provider run.

## 6. Guardrails

All online guardrails are deterministic JavaScript and execute for every applicable operation before publication. Guard failure closes only the affected source/partition as required, emits a bounded category-only diagnostic, and never falls back to model confidence, an LLM judge, stale truth, or partial output.

### Online guardrails

| Guardrail | Runs when | Code-enforced trigger | Fail-closed action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current authority and partition fence | Before/after content read and provider call; before each stage/write/query/publish | Exact partition/source/fingerprint/authority epoch differs, access is absent, or operation signal is aborted | Abort fetch/backoff, reject delayed output, discard/repair staging, return closed state; make no query projection. |
| Static prompt and no-tool boundary | Before provider request | System prompt/version is not the trusted constant; source-derived bytes appear outside the JSON data envelope; request contains tools, callbacks, URLs, storage/MCP handles, or prior conversation | Make 0 provider calls and withhold the source with a fixed boundary reason. |
| Input minimization and configured-provider gate | Before each call and generation | Provider/model differs from current FSB settings; missing settings; >8 excerpts/call, >24,000 characters/call, >8 normal calls, >192,000 characters/generation, or >1 repair | Do not call or retry; source remains pending/withheld. Never choose a fallback or evict another source. |
| Raw-response and bare-JSON gate | Immediately after response, before parsing | Empty/non-string, >128 KiB, prose/fence/trailing text, parse error, requested output cap >2,048 tokens, or cancelled/stale completion | Retain no raw output. Permit one fresh-authority repair only for parse/schema failure; otherwise discard generation. |
| Closed schema and semantic allowlists | Before any staging | Draft 2020-12 failure, unknown/non-plain data, cap violation, duplicate/dangling ref, disallowed kind/predicate, invalid endpoint matrix, confidence/executable field, or cross-batch unresolved ref | Stage 0 candidates from that response; bounded category/path diagnostic only; whole generation remains invisible. |
| Evidence/citation registry | Before candidate admission and again before publish | Excerpt ID absent from this request, `start >= end`, range outside excerpt, bytes do not match, material evidence is clipped in a fixture-defined required span, or locator belongs to another source/fingerprint | Reject response/generation. Never synthesize, repair, or search another source for evidence. |
| Atomic replacement and absence proof | On changed/deleted/revoked source and final publish | Prior fragment/posting/edge/cache remains; staging ledger incomplete; derived indexes mismatch; active pointer would precede payload writes | Keep partition/source read-fenced, resume/repair bounded purge, and publish no replacement until absence plus complete staging are proven pointer-last. |
| Cancellation/deadline fence | At every awaited boundary, provider retry sleep, storage mutation, and final currentness check | Operation signal aborted, absolute deadline elapsed, mutation guard/epoch stale, or a late promise resolves | Abort network work, stop retries, roll back or complete terminal repair, and guarantee 0 late durable/query-visible effects. |
| Storage integrity, quota, and recovery | On read, stage, index build, publish, and MV3 wake | Parse/checksum/reference error, write rejection/quota failure, orphaned/incomplete journal, or recovery-step cap reached | Withdraw affected truth, discard invalid staging, rebuild only derivable indexes, and report `recovery-pending`; never keep changed-source truth or evict another source. |
| Bounded query/facade admission | On exact lookup, lexical search, traversal, or provenance inspection | Caller lacks fresh exact-source/set authority; partition mismatches; depth/result/byte cap exceeded; raw shard/record requested | Return fail-quiet/closed bounded projection, never a partial cross-boundary fallback. |

### Offline flywheel

| Signal | Review loop | Promotion rule |
|---|---|---|
| Any deterministic fixture regression | Run all 37 fixtures plus full `npm test` on every Phase 55 code/prompt/schema change. | No merge/release until every Critical threshold returns to its exact target and the regression has a minimal fixture. |
| Validation, abstention, repair, provider-failure, or recovery rate crosses a local threshold | Review category-only counters; reproduce with a newly authored synthetic/redacted fixture rather than copying production text. | Prompt/schema/code changes require expert relabeling where domain semantics changed and a full gate rerun. |
| Domain-label disagreement or newly observed qualifier/amendment pattern | Counsel/legal-ops adjudicate the synthetic case and revise the gold-label version. | Add only after dual review; never weaken evidence precision or abstention gates to improve recall. |
| Optional configured-provider quality drift | Run the synthetic positive/negative corpus locally before approving a documented provider/model combination. | Require the provider-quality thresholds in Section 5; failure affects that local qualification, never triggers an automatic provider switch. |
| Optional LLM-judge experiment | Compare synthetic-fixture scores with expert labels offline. | Kappa >=0.80 and zero Critical false passes permit advisory use only; judges never become an online guard or publication authority. |

No production contract excerpt, filename, party, source identifier, locator text, raw model response, credential, or prompt is copied into an eval corpus or flywheel queue.

## 7. Production Monitoring

Monitoring is local and metadata-only. A new bounded graph diagnostic ledger/counter record lives in the graph store's existing trusted `chrome.storage.local` partition and is readable only after fresh exact-partition authority. It follows the repository's defensive-copy, finite-key, FIFO/byte-cap, and redaction patterns; it is purged with the partition and never exported automatically. Existing token/cost analytics may receive only provider/model, token counts, success, and the existing fixed source category `ai-provider`; it must not receive source metadata. The partition-owned ledger's operation-class enum distinguishes graph extraction without expanding the analytics payload.

Phoenix and hosted tracing are intentionally not configured. This is a direct override of the generic Phoenix default because a tracing service, telemetry export, or Python/server runtime would violate `LOCAL-01`, `LOCAL-02`, `LOCAL-03`, and the accepted Phase 55 boundary.

### Allowed diagnostic shape and retention

Each attempt record is an exact-key plain object containing only: schema/prompt version, provider/model identifiers, operation class, outcome/reason enum, excerpt/call/record/relation counts, input/output token counts when supplied by the provider, duration/retry/repair counts, validation keyword plus bounded schema **JSON-pointer paths**, recovery state, and a coarse timestamp. It contains no values from those paths. Per-partition records are capped at 100 entries and 64 KiB and expire after 30 days; aggregate counters are finite enums and saturating integers. Reads require current partition authority. Missing/revoked authority returns no existence signal, and partition withdrawal removes the ledger.

Forbidden in every metric, log, error, assertion message, and export: excerpt or locator text, raw prompt/output, filenames, source IDs/fingerprints, party/vendor/person names, account labels or permission IDs, credentials, URLs from source data, citations, and any indication that a source exists outside the currently authorized partition. Tests seed unique forbidden markers into every such field and require **zero marker occurrences** across storage snapshots, console/logger captures, errors, and facade results.

### Metrics and exact local thresholds

| Metric / invariant | Window and threshold | Local response |
|---|---|---|
| `unsafe_publication_total`, `publication_without_provenance_total`, `cross_partition_accept_total`, `stale_visibility_total` | Lifetime target **0**; any value >0 is Critical | Close affected partition/source, prevent projection, retain only the fixed category counter, and require a blocking synthetic regression fixture before release. |
| `absence_proof_failure_total` or `late_write_after_cancel_total` | Target **0**; any event is Critical | Keep reads fenced and enter bounded recovery; do not acknowledge completion or publish until terminal repair proves absence. |
| Per-call envelope | excerpts <=8, excerpt characters <=24,000, raw response text <=131,072 characters, requested output <=2,048 tokens, records <=128, relations <=256 | Any max-plus-one attempt is blocked before the next durable/provider effect and increments one bounded reason counter. |
| Per-generation envelope | normal calls <=8, repair calls <=1, excerpt characters <=192,000 | Overrun withholds the whole generation as budget-exceeded; no automatic fallback or partial publish. |
| Provider failure rate | Warning when >25% over the most recent 20 attempts (minimum 10) | Show only a local degraded/pending reason; preserve provider choice and ask for no new setup. |
| Parse/schema/semantic rejection rate | Warning when >10% over the most recent 20 attempts (minimum 10); Critical quality investigation when >=50% | Continue to withhold each invalid generation, run the synthetic suite, and review prompt/schema/provider compatibility; never relax the gate automatically. |
| Repair rate | Warning when >20% over the most recent 20 attempts (minimum 10) | Investigate with synthetic fixtures; one-repair cap remains unchanged. |
| Provider latency | Warning when p95 >18,000 ms over the most recent 20 calls; hard timeout remains 20,000 ms/call and the Phase 54 absolute operation deadline still governs | Preserve cancellation/timeout behavior; record duration only and do not retry beyond the allowed provider policy. |
| Quota/corruption/recovery | Any quota/corruption event warns; `recovery-pending` after the next bounded wake/recovery pass is Critical for that source/partition | Keep affected truth absent, discard staging or rebuild derivable indexes, and never evict another source. |
| Optional provider-quality synthetic run | Evidence precision 100%, recall >=90%, kind/predicate accuracy >=95%, insufficient/adversarial abstention 100% | Mark only that local provider/model qualification as failed; never send production data for diagnosis or switch providers automatically. |

### Sampling strategy

- Run code guardrails and increment safe aggregate outcomes on **100%** of operations; there is no sampled bypass.
- Keep only the bounded last 100 metadata-only attempt records per authorized partition; counts, sizes, durations, and outcomes are sufficient for rate and p95 calculations.
- Run all 37 deterministic fixtures in focused CI and the full regression suite on every prompt/schema/extractor/store/query change.
- Run configured-provider quality evaluation only on explicit local request and only with the synthetic corpus. Do not sample production source text automatically. When a production counter suggests a new failure mode, a human creates a synthetic/redacted reproduction before it enters the dataset.
- Review the reference corpus and thresholds at each prompt/schema version change and before milestone release. A privacy/security reviewer rechecks the diagnostic allowlist and forbidden-marker test whenever the record shape changes.
